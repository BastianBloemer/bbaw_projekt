**Autor:** Jiaxin Wu, Shanshan Huang, Zherui Zhang, Yuan Wang, Shu Wang, Ming Liu, Guoxin Su, Li Liu
**Datum:** 2026-10-01

**Zusammenfassung**

Knowledge graph (KG) construction is a fundamental task for organizing structured knowledge and supporting intelligent applications. Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) have substantially enhanced KG construction by improving semantic understanding, information extraction, and reasoning, thereby driving a shift from traditional pipeline-based methods toward LLM-driven frameworks. This transformation calls for a timely and systematic survey. In this survey, we provide a comprehensive review of the entire lifecycle of LLM-based KG construction, covering three core tasks: triple extraction (TE), knowledge fusion (KF), and knowledge graph completion (KGC). We further propose a unified taxonomy based on LLM integration strategies, categorizing existing methods into three paradigms: prompt engineering, parameter fine-tuning, and hybrid model collaboration. Based on this taxonomy, we systematically analyze representative techniques and their application scenarios across different stages of KG construction. In addition, we summarize 16 publicly available datasets and three widely used evaluation metrics, i.e., precision, recall, and F1-score, and discuss evaluation challenges in LLM-based KG construction. Through comparative experiments on 28 representative methods, we further analyze the strengths and limitations of different paradigms. We also discuss the extension of LLMs to domain-specific and multimodal KG construction. Finally, we summarize the current challenges and opportunities in this field and outline promising directions for future research.
